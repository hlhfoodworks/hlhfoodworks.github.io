Baked Stuffed French Toast

🔗 Link

Serves 6

Source: From Susan Muhlheim

Ingredients

Bread base

  • 4 large slices sourdough bread, crusts removed and cubed

Filling

  • 8 oz cream cheese, cubed
  • 1 large Granny Smith apple, peeled and chopped

Custard

  • 6 eggs
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons cinnamon

To finish

  • Powdered sugar

Steps

  1. Layer half the bread cubes in an ungreased 11x7 baking dish. Distribute cream cheese and apple over the bread, then top with remaining bread cubes.
  2. Whisk eggs, milk, and cinnamon together; pour evenly over the bread.
  3. Bake at 375° for 35 minutes until puffed and golden.
  4. Sprinkle with powdered sugar before serving.

Baked German Pancake (or Dutch Babies)

🔗 Link

One 12-inch pancake, or four 6-inch Dutch babies | Cook: 15-25 minutes

Source: The Breakfast Book, by Marion Cunningham

Ingredients

For the pan

  • Butter, for greasing the pan(s)

Batter

  • 3 eggs, room temperature
  • 1/2 cup milk
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons butter, melted

To finish

  • 2 tablespoons lemon juice
  • Confectioners' sugar, for dusting

Steps

  1. Preheat the oven to 450°F. Butter one 12-inch ovenproof skillet, or four 6-inch skillets or pans with ovenproof handles.
  2. Beat the eggs in a mixing bowl until thoroughly combined, then add the milk and blend well.
  3. Sift the flour and salt over the egg mixture, whisking steadily until smooth. Add the melted butter and mix briskly until combined.
  4. Pour the batter into the pan(s). Bake for 15 minutes at 450°F for small pancakes; for one large pancake, bake 15 minutes at 450°F then reduce to 350°F and bake 10 minutes more.
  5. Sprinkle the lemon juice over the pancake(s) and dust with confectioners' sugar. Serve at once, while puffed and hot.

Egg Strata

🔗 Link

Assemble the night before — ideal for brunch.
The amount of cheese is flexible; generous layers of cheddar work best.

Source: Family recipe card

Ingredients

Bread base

  • 8 slices bread (any kind)
  • Butter or margarine, for spreading

Custard

  • 4 eggs
  • 2 1/2 cups milk
  • 1/4 teaspoon dry mustard
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt

Cheese

  • Grated cheddar cheese, to taste (cheddar is best)

Steps

  1. Butter each slice of bread. Cut off crusts and cut into quarters. Butter a deep 9x13 Pyrex dish.
  2. Layer alternating bread and cheese in the dish.
  3. Beat together eggs, milk, mustard, and salt. Pour over bread and cheese. Let stand overnight in the refrigerator.
  4. Bake at 350°F for about 45 minutes.

★ Glazed Cinnamon Rolls (Tangzhong Version)

🔗 Link

One dozen round rolls | Cook: 15-17 minutes

This dough uses a tangzhong (a pre-cooked flour-and-milk paste) for a softer, more tender roll and better keeping quality.
The filling here is doubled from the original for a more generous swirl, the glaze includes optional cinnamon and food coloring.

Source: The Breakfast Book, by Marion Cunningham, adapted to use a tangzhong (see https://www.theperfectloaf.com/how-to-make-tangzhong/)

Ingredients

Tangzhong

  • 1/4 cup (30g) all-purpose flour
  • 2/3 cup (160g) milk

Yeast

  • 1/4 cup warm water
  • 1 package active dry yeast
  • 1 teaspoon sugar

Dough

  • 3 3/4 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 cup sugar
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 pound (2 sticks) butter, chilled
  • 3 egg yolks
  • 1/3 cup plus 2 tablespoons milk (the reserved 1/3 cup from the tangzhong step, plus 2 tablespoons extra)

Filling

  • 1/2 cup (1 stick) butter, melted
  • 3/4 cup (12 tablespoons) sugar
  • 2 teaspoons cinnamon

Glaze

  • 1 1/2 cups confectioners' sugar
  • 2 tablespoons butter, room temperature
  • 2 tablespoons water
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cinnamon (optional)
  • Food coloring (optional, to taste)

Steps

  1. Tangzhong: In a small saucepan, whisk together the 1/4 cup (30g) flour and 2/3 cup (160g) milk until smooth. Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly, until thickened to a paste, about 2 minutes. Scrape into a small bowl; reserve 1/3 cup for the dough and discard the rest (or save for another use).
  2. Yeast: Put the warm water in a small bowl and sprinkle the yeast over. Stir in the 1 teaspoon sugar and let stand until foamy, about 5 minutes.
  3. Dry mix: In the bowl of a food processor, combine the flour, sugar, and salt; pulse briefly to blend. Add the chilled butter and pulse until the mixture resembles coarse crumbs.
  4. Dough: Transfer the flour-butter mixture to the bowl of a stand mixer fitted with the paddle attachment. Add the yeast mixture, the tangzhong, the egg yolks, and the 1/3 cup plus 2 tablespoons milk. Mix on medium speed until a smooth dough forms.
  5. Filling:
    • Combine the melted butter, sugar, and cinnamon in a small bowl to make a spreadable filling.
    • On a clean, floured countertop, roll out the whole batch of dough into one large, thin rectangle about 18-20 inches by 24 inches.
    • Spread the filling mixture evenly over the rectangle.
    • Using a pizza cutter, cut the rectangle lengthwise into 12 long strips. Roll each strip up individually into a spiral and place in a greased pan.
  6. Cover loosely and let rise for 1 hour. Meanwhile, make the glaze: Sift the confectioners' sugar into a small bowl, then stir in the butter, water, and optional cinnamon until smooth. Tint with food coloring if desired.
  7. Bake in a preheated 400°F oven for 15-17 minutes. Remove the rolls and set them on a rack over a piece of waxed paper.
  8. Spoon the prepared glaze over each roll while still hot.

★ Homemade Biscuits

🔗 Link

8 large biscuits | Prep: 15 minutes | Cook: 15 to 20 minutes

Cut into 8 big squares rather than small rounds, so these come out extra generous.
Whole milk plus a splash of white vinegar stands in for buttermilk here — no need to track down real buttermilk.

Source: Adapted from Inspired Taste (inspiredtaste.net), by Adam and Joanne Gallagher

Ingredients

Soured milk

  • 1 3/4 cups whole milk
  • 1 tablespoon white vinegar

Dry ingredients

  • 4 cups (520 g) all-purpose flour, spooned and leveled
  • 10 teaspoons (3 tablespoons plus 1 teaspoon) aluminum-free baking powder, leveled
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 teaspoons fine sea salt

Butter

  • 12 tablespoons (1 1/2 sticks; 170 g) cold butter

Steps

  1. Make the soured milk: Stir the vinegar into the whole milk. Let stand at least 5 minutes while you prepare the dough (it will look slightly curdled — that's fine).
  2. Heat the oven to 425°F. Set aside an oven-safe 10- to 12-inch skillet, preferably cast iron, or a cookie sheet lined with parchment.
  3. Dry ingredients: In a food processor, combine the flour, baking powder, baking soda, sugar, and salt. Pulse 3 to 4 times to combine.
  4. Cut the cold butter into small cubes or thin slices; scatter over the flour mixture. Pulse 5 to 7 times, just until the butter is broken into pea-sized pieces.
  5. Transfer to a large bowl. Make a well in the middle; pour in the soured milk and stir gently with a fork or spatula just until the dough comes together (some dry bits are fine — don't overmix).
  6. Transfer the dough to a lightly floured work surface. Sprinkle a little flour over the top and gently bring the dough together with your hands.
  7. Fold the dough:
    • Without overworking the dough, gently pat it into a rough rectangle about 3/4 inch thick.
    • Fold the dough into thirds, envelope-style — bring one short side over the center, then fold the opposite side over it, like a letter.
    • Rotate the dough 90 degrees, then repeat the pat-and-fold two more times to build flaky layers.
  8. Pat the dough into a rough 3/4-inch-thick rectangle. Cut into 8 squares — no need to cut rounds or reroll scraps.
  9. Arrange the squares close together on the skillet (or parchment-lined cookie sheet) to help them rise tall.
  10. Bake 15 to 20 minutes, until the tops are deeply golden brown and the biscuits have risen tall.

Raised Waffles

🔗 Link

About 8 waffles

The batter keeps well in the refrigerator for several days.

Source: The Breakfast Book, by Marion Cunningham (originally from an early Fannie Farmer cookbook)

Ingredients

Yeast

  • 1/2 cup warm water
  • 1 package dry yeast

Batter (overnight)

  • 2 cups milk, warmed
  • 1/2 cup (1 stick) butter, melted
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 2 cups all-purpose flour

Morning additions

  • 2 eggs
  • 1/4 teaspoon baking soda

Steps

  1. Yeast: In a large mixing bowl (the batter will rise to double its volume), put the warm water and sprinkle the yeast over. Let stand until foamy, about 5 minutes.
  2. Add the milk, butter, salt, sugar, and flour to the yeast mixture; beat until smooth and blended (a hand-rotary beater works well). Cover tightly and leave overnight at room temperature.
  3. Just before cooking, beat in the eggs and baking soda; stir until well mixed. The batter will be very thin.
  4. Pour about 1/2 to 3/4 cup batter into a very hot waffle iron. Bake until golden and crisp.

Chinese Tomato Egg Stir-fry

🔗 Link

2

Source: Adapted from The Woks of Life (thewoksoflife.com)

Ingredients

Eggs

  • 4 large eggs
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon sesame oil
  • 1/8 teaspoon white pepper

Sauce

  • 3 medium tomatoes (about 1 pound), cut into wedges
  • 2 scallions, white and green parts separated, sliced
  • 3 tablespoons neutral oil, divided
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon sugar
  • 2 teaspoons ketchup
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons soy sauce

Steps

  1. Beat eggs with 1/4 teaspoon salt, sesame oil, and white pepper until well combined.
  2. Heat 2 tablespoons oil in a wok or nonstick skillet over medium-high heat. Add eggs and scramble gently, folding rather than stirring, until just set but still slightly wet. Transfer to a plate.
  3. Add remaining 1 tablespoon oil to wok. Add scallion whites and stir-fry 30 seconds. Add tomatoes, 1/2 teaspoon salt, and sugar. Cook over medium-high heat, stirring occasionally, until tomatoes break down and release their juices, about 3 minutes.
  4. Stir in ketchup and soy sauce.
  5. Return eggs and scallion greens to wok. Toss gently to combine. Serve immediately over rice.
