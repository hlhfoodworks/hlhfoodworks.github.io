Baked Crunchy Hot Honey Chicken

🔗 Link

Serves 6 | Prep: 15 minutes | Cook: 25 minutes | Total: 40 minutes

Source: Half Baked Harvest, by Tieghan Gerard

Ingredients

Crumb coating

  • 6 cups cornflakes (gluten-free if needed)
  • 1/4 cup grated parmesan cheese
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon onion powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder

Egg wash

  • 2 large eggs, beaten
  • 2 tablespoons hot sauce

Chicken

  • 2 pounds chicken breast tenderloins
  • Extra-virgin olive oil, for drizzling

Hot honey sauce

  • 1/2 cup honey
  • 2 to 3 tablespoons hot sauce
  • 1 to 3 teaspoons cayenne pepper
  • 3/4 teaspoon chipotle chili powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon onion powder
  • Sea salt

To finish

  • Fresh thyme, cilantro, or parsley

Steps

  1. Preheat the oven to 425°F. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper.
  2. Pulse the cornflakes, parmesan, paprika, onion powder, garlic powder, and a pinch of salt in a food processor until fine crumbs form. Transfer to a shallow bowl.
  3. Beat the eggs with the hot sauce in a bowl; add the chicken and toss well to coat.
  4. Dredge the chicken through the crumbs, covering fully. Place on the baking sheet. For a thicker coating, dip back through the egg wash and crumbs a second time.
  5. Drizzle the chicken with olive oil. Bake 20 to 25 minutes, until crisp all around.
  6. Just before the chicken is done, warm the honey, hot sauce, cayenne, chili powder, onion powder, and garlic powder with a pinch of salt in a small saucepan over low heat until combined.
  7. Drizzle the warm sauce over the chicken. Top with fresh herbs.

Brown Butter Sage Skillet Chicken

🔗 Link

Serves 4 | Prep: 10 minutes | Cook: 20 minutes | Total: 30 minutes

Thin-sliced chicken breasts cook fastest; if starting with standard breasts, slice horizontally through the middle for thinner cutlets.
Great served over egg noodles, orzo, or a light pasta.

Source: Nourish and Fete, by Monica

Ingredients

Chicken

  • 4 boneless, skinless chicken breasts, sliced or pounded thin
  • Kosher salt and black pepper
  • 1/4 cup all-purpose flour

Oil and butter

  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter (of the 6 tablespoons below)
  • 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil

Brown butter sauce

  • 4 tablespoons unsalted butter (the remainder of the 6 tablespoons total)
  • 1/3 cup fresh sage leaves
  • Juice of 1/2 lemon (reserve the other half for serving)
  • 1/2 cup white wine or low-sodium chicken broth

To serve

  • Lemon wedges

Steps

  1. Season both sides of the chicken generously with salt and pepper. Place the flour in a shallow bowl or plate and dredge each cutlet, shaking off the excess.
  2. Add 2 tablespoons of the butter and the olive oil to a large skillet over medium-high heat. When the butter melts, add the chicken and cook until golden brown, about 3 to 4 minutes per side. Transfer to a plate.
  3. Add the remaining 4 tablespoons butter to the skillet. When melted, add the sage leaves. Let the butter brown and bubble until it smells nutty and the sage is crispy, about 2 minutes.
  4. Add the lemon juice and wine (or broth) to the skillet. Simmer 2 to 3 minutes, scraping up any browned bits from the bottom. Return the chicken to the skillet and warm through. Serve with lemon wedges.

Chili Crisp Chicken n' Peanuts Scoop

🔗 Link

Serves 6 | Active: 15–20 minutes

A not-so-classic chicken salad made spicy with chili crisp and nutty with peanut butter — no cooking required. Be gentle when mixing; the acid in the dressing can break down the chicken if you overwork it. A few careful folds are all it takes.
Excellent served over pan-fried udon noodles.

Source: Molly Baz

Ingredients

Prep

  • 1 pound rotisserie chicken, pulled into large but bite-sized pieces (about 2 heaping cups)
  • 2 small Persian cucumbers (about 5–6 oz), finely chopped
  • 1½ teaspoons fresh ginger, finely grated (no need to peel)
  • 3 scallions, whites and greens thinly sliced

Peanut dressing

  • Heaping ⅓ cup crunchy peanut butter
  • 2 tablespoons rice vinegar, plus more to taste
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 2–4 tablespoons chili crisp, plus more to taste
  • Honey, to taste (optional)
  • Water, added 1 teaspoon at a time to reach thick but pourable consistency

To serve

  • Fresh mint
  • Toasted sesame seeds
  • Additional grated ginger and chili crisp, if desired

Steps

  1. Prep the components:
    • Pull 1 pound rotisserie chicken into large but bite-sized pieces (about 2 heaping cups).
    • Finely chop the cucumbers. Finely grate the ginger (no need to peel). Thinly slice the scallion whites and greens.
  2. Make the peanut dressing:
    • In a large bowl, whisk together the peanut butter, 2 tablespoons rice vinegar, grated ginger, and 1 teaspoon salt.
    • Add chili crisp a tablespoon at a time, stirring, until the desired spice level is reached (you'll likely use 2–4 tablespoons). Add honey to taste, if desired.
    • Stir in water a teaspoon at a time until the dressing is thick but pourable.
    • Add the pulled chicken, diced cucumber, and sliced scallions; fold gently to coat. Do not overmix — you want to retain the texture of the chicken. Season to taste with salt and additional rice vinegar.
  3. Serve topped with mint, toasted sesame seeds, and additional grated ginger and chili crisp if desired.

★ Company Baked Chicken

🔗 Link

Marked "v. good!" on the original card.

Source: Family recipe card, credited to Grandmother Brenda

Ingredients

Chicken

  • 2 chickens or equivalent amount of chicken parts
  • Black pepper
  • Garlic powder

Marinade

  • 1/2 cup honey
  • 1/2 cup soy sauce
  • 3 tablespoons oil

To finish

  • Sesame seeds

Steps

  1. Season the chicken pieces all over with pepper and garlic powder.
  2. Combine the honey, soy sauce, and oil to make the marinade.
  3. Add the chicken to the marinade, turning to coat. Cover and marinate in the refrigerator 24 hours, turning frequently.
  4. Preheat the oven to 350°F.
  5. Place the chicken skin-side down in a baking dish with the marinade. Bake uncovered 45 minutes.
  6. Turn the chicken pieces over, sprinkle with sesame seeds, and continue baking another 30 minutes, basting occasionally with the pan juices.

Creamy Spinach-Artichoke Chicken Stew

🔗 Link

Serves 4 to 6 | Total: 50 minutes

Source: NYT Cooking, by Sarah DiGregorio

Ingredients

Aromatics

  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 1 large yellow or red onion, finely chopped
  • Kosher salt and black pepper
  • 3 celery stalks, chopped
  • 8 garlic cloves, smashed and chopped

Braise

  • 2 cups chicken stock
  • 3/4 cup white wine
  • 2 to 2 1/4 pounds boneless, skinless chicken thighs
  • 1/2 lemon, juiced (about 1 1/2 tablespoons)
  • 1 teaspoon Aleppo pepper or red pepper flakes

Spinach and artichokes

  • 1 (10-ounce) package frozen cut spinach
  • 1 (12-ounce) jar marinated artichoke hearts, drained (about 1 heaping cup)

To finish

  • 1/2 cup cream cheese (about 4 ounces)
  • 1/2 cup finely chopped fresh dill
  • 4 to 6 scallions, thinly sliced
  • Grated Parmesan cheese

Steps

  1. Aromatics:
    • In a large Dutch oven over medium-high heat, melt the butter. Add the onion, season lightly with salt, and cook, stirring, until softened, about 5 minutes.
    • Add the celery and cook, stirring, until softened, about 5 minutes, adjusting the heat as needed to avoid scorching. Stir in the garlic and cook 1 minute more.
  2. Pour in the stock and wine, and bring to a bubble. Add the chicken thighs, lemon juice, and Aleppo pepper or red pepper flakes.
  3. Reduce the heat to maintain a low simmer and simmer, uncovered, 20 minutes.
  4. Add the frozen spinach and artichoke hearts. Increase the heat to medium-high and cook, stirring, to help the spinach defrost and the liquid reduce slightly, about 5 minutes.
  5. Add the cream cheese in dollops, stirring to melt it into the stew. Add the dill. Using two forks, coarsely break or shred the chicken into pieces. Taste and adjust seasoning.
  6. Divide among bowls and top with scallions and Parmesan.

Crispy Chicken With Lime Butter

🔗 Link

Serves 4 | Prep: 5 minutes | Cook: 35 minutes | Total: 40 minutes

The rendered chicken fat (schmaltz) can be saved to pan-fry vegetables, enrich a soup or sauce, or spread on toast.

Source: NYT Cooking, by Eric Kim

Ingredients

Chicken

  • 4 large bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (about 2 pounds)
  • Salt and pepper
  • 1 tablespoon peanut or canola oil

Aromatics

  • 2 garlic cloves, crushed

Pan sauce

  • 1/2 cup chicken stock or 1/4 cup water
  • 2 tablespoons fresh lime juice, plus wedges for serving
  • 2 teaspoons maple syrup
  • 3 tablespoons cold unsalted butter, cut into pats

To serve

  • Parsley, cilantro, basil, or mint leaves (optional)

Steps

  1. Pat the chicken dry and season with salt and pepper. If you have time, let sit at room temperature 10 to 30 minutes.
  2. Heat a large skillet over medium heat. Add the oil and swirl to coat the pan. Place the chicken skin side down and cook undisturbed until the skin is deeply golden and crispy, about 20 to 25 minutes.
  3. Add the garlic to the pan. Flip the chicken and cook until the bottom is lightly browned and the meat is cooked through, about 5 to 8 minutes more. Transfer to a plate.
  4. Pan sauce: Add the chicken stock, lime juice, and maple syrup to the skillet. Season with salt and pepper. Bring to a simmer and cook until slightly reduced, about 2 minutes. Remove from heat and whisk in the cold butter a pat at a time until the sauce is glossy.
  5. Serve the chicken with the pan sauce, lime wedges, and the optional fresh herbs (lightly spritzed with lime juice and seasoned with flaky salt, if using).

Expertly Spiced and Glazed Roast Turkey

🔗 Link

Serves 8–10 | Active: 45 min | Total: About 2 hrs (plus 24–48 hrs dry-brining)

Turkey is spatchcocked for more even roasting. Begin 24–48 hours ahead for the dry brine.
Option: after removing the backbone, also separate the wings and legs for even faster, more even cooking as individual pieces — see Step 1.
Requires a spice mill or mortar and pestle.

Source: https://www.epicurious.com/recipes/food/views/expertly-spiced-and-glazed-roast-turkey

Ingredients

Spice rub

  • 2 tablespoons whole black peppercorns
  • 2 tablespoons whole pink or black peppercorns
  • 1/2 cup Diamond Crystal or 1/4 cup plus 1 1/2 teaspoons Morton kosher salt
  • 2 tablespoons garlic powder
  • 2 tablespoons onion powder
  • 1 tablespoon smoked paprika
  • 1 tablespoon light brown sugar

Turkey

  • 1 (12-14 pounds) turkey, neck and giblets removed

Glaze

  • Small handful hardy herbs (sage, rosemary, bay leaves, and/or thyme)
  • 4 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 2 (2x1") strips orange zest
  • 1/3 cup low-sodium soy sauce
  • 1/3 cup sherry vinegar or red wine vinegar
  • 1/3 cup (packed) light brown sugar

For roasting

  • 6 tablespoons neutral oil (vegetable, peanut, or canola)
  • 1 cup water, plus more as needed

Steps

  1. Spatchcock the turkey (preferred method):
    • Place turkey breast side down. Using kitchen shears, cut along one side of the backbone all the way through, then cut along the other side to remove the backbone entirely. Save for stock.
    • Flip turkey breast side up. Press firmly on the breastbone with the heel of your hand until it cracks and the bird lies flat.
    • Option — full breakdown: after removing the backbone, also separate the wings (grip each wing, pull outward, cut through the joint) and the legs (cut through skin, pull back until ball joint pops out, cut through joint). You'll have 5 separate pieces: 2 wings, 2 legs, 1 breast.
  2. Make the spice rub: Finely grind both peppercorns in a spice mill or mortar and pestle. Transfer to a bowl and mix in kosher salt, garlic powder, onion powder, paprika, and 1 tablespoon brown sugar.
  3. Place turkey on a wire rack set inside a rimmed baking sheet. Sprinkle spice rub liberally all over, patting to adhere (you may not use all of it). Refrigerate uncovered at least 24 hours and up to 2 days.
  4. Remove turkey from rack; rinse baking sheet and rack if needed. Line baking sheet with 3 layers of foil, set rack back inside, and return turkey skin side up. Let sit at room temperature 2–3 hours.
  5. Meanwhile, make the glaze: Cook herbs, garlic, orange zest, soy sauce, vinegar, and 1/3 cup brown sugar in a small saucepan over medium heat, stirring occasionally, until sugar dissolves and glaze thickens slightly (barely coats a spoon), 10–12 minutes. Remove from heat.
  6. Place a rack in the middle of the oven; preheat to 425°F. Rub turkey all over with oil and pour 1 cup water into the baking sheet. Roast, rotating baking sheet halfway through, until skin is mostly golden brown, 20–25 minutes.
  7. Reduce oven to 300°F. Continue roasting, brushing with glaze every 20 minutes and adding more water by 1/2-cupfuls as needed to keep some liquid in the pan, until an instant-read thermometer reads 150°F in the thickest part of the breast and 170°F in the thickest part of the thigh, 50–70 minutes more (total time 1–1 1/2 hours; fully broken-down pieces may finish faster). Skin should be deep golden brown and shiny.
  8. Transfer to a cutting board, tent loosely with foil, and rest 30–60 minutes before carving.

Source: Adapted from Pampered Chef, 29 Minutes to Dinner

Ingredients

Shrimp

  • ½ pound shrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 1 tablespoon vegetable oil
  • ¼ teaspoon salt
  • ¼ teaspoon pepper
  • ⅛ teaspoon sugar

Orzo

  • 2 cups chicken broth
  • 1 cup white wine
  • 1 clove garlic, minced
  • 8 oz orzo

Finish

  • 1 cup frozen peas
  • 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice
  • 1 tablespoon unsalted butter

To serve

  • 1 tablespoon lemon zest, grated
  • 1 tablespoon fresh parsley, finely chopped
  • Freshly grated Parmesan (optional)

Steps

  1. In a large bowl, toss shrimp with salt, pepper, and sugar. Heat oil in a large skillet over medium-high. Cook shrimp 3 minutes. Turn and remove from heat; let stand 1 minute until pink and opaque. Remove shrimp from skillet.
  2. Add broth, wine, garlic, and orzo to the skillet. Bring to a boil. Cover and cook on medium-low until orzo is tender, 10–12 minutes.
  3. Return shrimp to skillet; add peas, butter, and lemon juice. Cover and let stand 3–5 minutes until heated through. Remove from heat and top with lemon zest and parsley. Serve with freshly grated Parmesan if desired.

Spicy Grilled Shrimp

🔗 Link

Serves 4 | Total: 20 min

Source: Mark Bittman, NYT Cooking (1999)

Ingredients

Paste and shrimp

  • 1 large clove garlic
  • 1 tablespoon coarse salt
  • ½ teaspoon cayenne
  • 1 teaspoon paprika
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 2 teaspoons fresh-squeezed lemon juice
  • 1½–2 pounds large shrimp (15–18 per pound), peeled, rinsed, and dried

To serve

  • Lemon wedges

Steps

  1. Start a charcoal or gas grill, or heat the broiler. Make the fire as hot as it will get and put the rack close to the heat source.
  2. Mince garlic with salt; mix with cayenne and paprika, then make into a paste with olive oil and lemon juice. Smear paste all over shrimp. Grill or broil 2–3 minutes per side, turning once. Serve immediately or at room temperature with lemon wedges.
  3. NOTE: For best results, make sure your paprika is fresh — if it doesn't taste vibrant and smoky, it's time to buy more.

Stuffed Eggplant Creole

🔗 Link

Serves 4

Crawfish, ground beef, or tofu can be substituted for shrimp.
Lowfat sour cream or plain nonfat yogurt both work well.

Source: Family recipe card, credited to Susan Muhlheim

Ingredients

Eggplant

  • 2 small eggplants (1 pound each)

Filling

  • 1 tablespoon vegetable oil
  • 1 pound shrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • 1/4 cup finely chopped onion
  • 1/4 cup finely chopped green pepper
  • 1/4 cup finely chopped celery
  • 1 (14-oz) can tomatoes, undrained
  • 1/4 teaspoon dried thyme
  • Salt and Tabasco to taste
  • 1 cup dried bread crumbs, divided
  • 1/2 cup lowfat sour cream or plain nonfat yogurt

Steps

  1. Preheat oven to 375°F. Cut eggplants in half lengthwise. Place in a large pan, cover with water, bring to a boil, cover, and simmer 15 minutes. Drain and cool.
  2. Scoop out pulp from eggplant, leaving a 1/4-inch shell intact. Set pulp aside.
  3. In a skillet, heat vegetable oil. Sauté garlic with shrimp. Add onion, green pepper, and celery; cook 5 minutes over low heat, stirring occasionally.
  4. Stir in tomatoes, salt, thyme, and Tabasco. Add half the bread crumbs, then the eggplant pulp and sour cream; stir well.
  5. Stuff mixture into the 4 eggplant shells. Top with remaining bread crumbs.
  6. Place in a baking dish and bake 30 minutes.

Sea Scallops with Red Peppers and Tomatoes

🔗 Link

Serves 4

Source: Family recipe, attributed to Grandmother Brenda

Ingredients

Scallops

  • 1 1/2 pounds sea scallops, cut crosswise in half
  • 1/4 cup milk
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • Flour for dredging

Sauce

  • 4 tablespoons olive oil (divided)
  • 1 sweet red pepper, cored, seeded, cut into 2" julienne pieces
  • 1 cup ripe plum tomatoes, seeded, peeled, cut into 1/2" pieces

Finish

  • 2 tablespoons finely chopped shallots
  • 1 tablespoon finely chopped garlic
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 4 tablespoons chopped parsley

For serving

  • Cooked rice

Steps

  1. Soak scallops in milk with salt and pepper for about 20 minutes.
  2. Heat 2 tablespoons olive oil in skillet. Add red pepper and tomatoes, season with salt and pepper, cook and stir until soft, 2-3 minutes. Set aside.
  3. Drain scallops and dredge in flour, shaking off excess.
  4. Heat remaining 2 tablespoons oil over high heat. Cook scallops in a single layer until golden.
  5. Add shallots, garlic, and lemon juice, shaking the pan. Cook 30 seconds.
  6. Add pepper and tomato mixture, stir and toss 2 minutes. Add parsley. Serve immediately over rice.

Shrimp Scampi with Linguini

Serves 4–6 | Prep: 15 min | Cook: 25 min | Total: 40 min

Source: Tyler Florence, Food Network (Food 911)

Ingredients

Pasta

  • 1 pound linguini

Shrimp and aromatics

  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
  • 2 shallots, finely diced
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • Pinch Aleppo pepper or red pepper flakes (optional)
  • 1 pound shrimp, peeled and deveined
  • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper

Sauce

  • ½ cup dry white wine
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil

To serve

  • ¼ cup finely chopped fresh parsley
  • Extra-virgin olive oil, for drizzling

Steps

  1. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Add linguini and cook 6–8 minutes until not quite done (it will finish in the sauce). Drain.
  2. Meanwhile, melt 2 tablespoons butter with 2 tablespoons olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Remove shrimp and set aside.
  3. Add wine and lemon juice to the pan; bring to a boil. Add remaining 2 tablespoons butter and 2 tablespoons olive oil; when butter has melted, return shrimp to the pan along with parsley and drained pasta. Toss well; season with salt and pepper. Drizzle with olive oil and serve immediately.

★ Moules Marinières

🔗 Link

Serves 4 | Prep: 45 min | Cook: 15 min | Total: 1 hour

Source: Florence Fabricant, NYT Cooking; garlic and cream option adapted from George Duran, Food Network

Ingredients

Mussels

  • 6 pounds mussels

Aromatics

  • 4 tablespoons unsalted butter, divided
  • 4 tablespoons finely minced shallots (about 2 medium shallots)
  • OPTIONAL: 2–4 garlic cloves, finely minced

Wine

  • 4 cups dry white wine

Cream (optional)

  • ½ cup heavy cream

To serve

  • Freshly ground black pepper
  • 2 tablespoons minced fresh parsley
  • Crusty bread

Steps

  1. Scrub the mussels under cold water. Pull off any beards (the fibrous tufts near the hinge). Discard any that are cracked or don't close when tapped.
  2. In a large pot (at least 8 quarts), melt 2 tablespoons of the butter over low heat. Add shallots (and garlic, if using) and sauté until soft but not browned, about 3–5 minutes.
  3. Add the wine; raise heat to medium. Add mussels, cover, and steam until they open, about 10–15 minutes.
  4. Using a slotted spoon, remove mussels from the pot; divide among warmed bowls. Discard any that did not open.
  5. FOR MOULES À LA CRÈME: Stir the cream into the remaining broth and simmer 2–3 minutes until slightly thickened.
  6. Taste the broth. If too salty, dilute with a little water. Add the remaining 2 tablespoons butter and swirl until melted. Season with pepper. Ladle broth over mussels, taking care not to disturb any sediment at the bottom of the pot.
  7. Sprinkle with parsley and serve immediately with crusty bread.
  8. TIP: After the meal, shuck and refrigerate any leftover mussels.

  5. Reheat wok. Add garlic, ginger, and cayenne; stir a few seconds. Add tomato sauce, vinegar, wine, sugar, salt, ¼ cup water, and the cornstarch slurry. Cook and stir until sauce thickens.

Sheet-Pan Italian Sub Dinner

🔗 Link

Serves 4 | Total: 25 min

All the flavors of an Italian sub — salami, radicchio, tomatoes, pepperoncini, chickpeas — roasted until caramelized and briny. Can swap in cauliflower florets, cubes of squash, or halved red potatoes for the radicchio.

Source: Ali Slagle, NYT Cooking

Ingredients

Vinaigrette

  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 2 tablespoons dried oregano
  • 2 teaspoons kosher salt
  • ¼ cup red wine vinegar
  • ¼ cup extra-virgin olive oil

Roasting ingredients

  • 1 (14-oz) can chickpeas, drained and patted dry
  • 5 oz salami (casing removed if present), cut into ½-inch pieces
  • 1 red onion, peeled, halved lengthwise, cut into ½-inch wedges
  • 1 medium head radicchio (about ¾ pound), quartered lengthwise
  • 1 cup cherry or grape tomatoes, halved
  • 8 pepperoncini peppers

To serve

  • ½ cup ricotta
  • Kosher salt and black pepper
  • Crusty bread

Steps

  1. Heat oven to 450°F. Coarsely chop the garlic, then add the dried oregano and 2 teaspoons salt on top and chop together until it forms a paste. Transfer to a bowl and stir in the vinegar and olive oil.
  2. Pour half the dressing into a large bowl. Add the chickpeas, salami, red onion, radicchio, tomatoes, and pepperoncini. Mix gently to coat, trying not to break up the radicchio and onion. Spread evenly on a baking sheet. Roast, tossing halfway through, until the red onions and chickpeas are golden, 15–20 minutes.
  3. Season the ricotta with salt and pepper. Serve the roasted mixture with crusty bread and a dollop of ricotta and extra vinaigrette, or assemble into sandwiches.

Peppered Duck Breast With Red Wine Sauce

🔗 Link

Serves 6 | Total: ~1½ hours (including 1 hour marinate)

A steak au poivre made with duck. Fancy enough for a gathering, relaxed enough for any night you want something special. The sauce can be made ahead and reheated, thinned with a little broth.

Source: David Tanis, NYT Cooking

Ingredients

Duck and marinade (up to 1 day ahead)

  • 3 Muscovy duck breasts (about 1 pound each)
  • Salt
  • 1 tablespoon freshly crushed black peppercorns (about 1 teaspoon per breast)
  • 4 garlic cloves, sliced
  • Fresh thyme sprigs

Sauce

  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, divided
  • Reserved duck tenderloins (from Step 1)
  • 1 large shallot, sliced
  • 1 tablespoon tomato paste
  • 1 small thyme sprig
  • 2 cups unsalted chicken broth
  • ¼ cup + 2 tablespoons medium-bodied red wine
  • 1 tablespoon Cognac or brandy
  • 2 teaspoons potato starch

Steps

  1. Remove the tenderloins (the thin strips on the undersides of the duck breasts) and reserve for the sauce. With a sharp knife, trim away any gristle from the undersides and any excess fat. Score the skin in a diamond pattern, cutting through the fat but not into the meat. Lightly sprinkle salt on both sides, then rub 1 teaspoon crushed black peppercorns all over each breast. Sprinkle with the garlic and a few thyme sprigs, cover, and leave at room temperature for 1 hour. (For deeper flavor, refrigerate for several hours or overnight, then return to room temperature before cooking.)
  2. Heat 1 tablespoon butter in a saucepan over medium heat. Add the shallot and reserved duck tenderloins and let them brown well, stirring occasionally, 5–8 minutes. Stir in the tomato paste and thyme sprig and let fry for 1 minute.
  3. Add the broth and ¼ cup wine, raise heat to a brisk simmer, and reduce to about 1¼ cups, about 10 minutes. Strain the sauce and return to heat. Add the remaining 2 tablespoons red wine and the Cognac or brandy and cook 1 minute more. Dissolve the potato starch in 2 tablespoons cold water, then stir into the sauce. Whisk in the remaining 1 tablespoon butter. Season with salt.
  4. Remove and discard the garlic and thyme from the breasts. Heat a large, heavy skillet over medium-high. When hot, lay in the duck breasts skin-side down and let sizzle. Lower heat to medium and cook for a total of 7 minutes, checking to ensure the skin isn't browning too quickly. Turn over and cook another 3 minutes for rare, 4–5 minutes for medium-rare. Transfer to a warm platter and rest 10 minutes. Slice crosswise at a slight angle and serve with the sauce.

Naomi's Nabe (Japanese Hot Pot)

🔗 Link

A reference card combining a magazine clipping and Naomi's handwritten ingredient selections. Nabe is a communal hot pot served at the table -- quantities are flexible; scale to your group.
Serve with Mitsukan shabu-shabu sesame sauce and citrus seasoned soy for dipping.

Source: From Naomi Shalowitz

Ingredients

Proteins

  • Chicken, thinly sliced
  • Shrimp, peeled and deveined
  • Salmon, cut into bite-size pieces
  • Firm tofu, cubed

Vegetables

  • Napa cabbage (hakusai), roughly chopped
  • Shungiku (chrysanthemum greens)
  • Enoki mushrooms
  • Shiitake mushrooms
  • Carrots, thinly sliced
  • Bok choy

Noodles

  • Cellophane noodles or soba

Rice

  • Nishiki medium-grain Japanese rice (1 1/2 cups water per 1 cup rice)

Broth

  • Chicken broth (enough to fill the pot)

Dipping sauces

  • Mitsukan shabu-shabu sesame sauce
  • Citrus seasoned soy sauce

Steps

  1. Cook rice according to package directions (1 1/2 cups water per 1 cup rice).
  2. Prepare all proteins and vegetables in bite-size pieces; arrange on platters for the table.
  3. Bring chicken broth to a gentle simmer in a hot pot or large shallow pot at the table.
  4. Dip proteins and vegetables into the broth; cook until done (chicken 2-3 minutes; shrimp and fish 1-2 minutes; vegetables 1-3 minutes depending on density). Skim the broth as needed.
  5. Eat with rice and dipping sauces. Add noodles partway through or at the end.
  6. Serve the enriched broth as a soup at the end of the meal.
